A 2 kg mass on an 18 N/m spring

What you should be able to do at the end: predict how the mass moves from the force law and its starting state, then check the prediction. You need two things first: Newton's second law, and the derivatives of sine and cosine.

Assumptions:

  • The spring is linear (Hooke's law) and has no mass.
  • There is no friction and no drive force.
  • x is measured from the equilibrium position. If the spring hangs vertically, gravity only moves the equilibrium position, so the model does not change.

The data:

  • m = 2 kg, k = 18 N/m
  • x(0) = +0.3 m: the spring is stretched.
  • v(0) = −0.6 m/s: the mass already moves toward equilibrium.

1. Predict before you calculate

At t = 0 the force is F = −kx = −18 × 0.3 = −5.4 N. The acceleration is −2.7 m/s². The velocity is negative too, so the force and the motion point the same way, and the mass speeds up toward equilibrium.

When the mass reaches x = 0 it is moving, so it goes past equilibrium. It went in with some speed, so it can go farther than 0.3 m on the other side.

Prediction:

  • The motion is an oscillation.
  • The amplitude is larger than 0.3 m.
  • The fastest speed occurs at x = 0. At that point the acceleration is zero.

2. Make the equation act, step by step (M1)

The equation of motion is m·x'' = −k·x, so x'' = −9·x. Read it as a rule:

  • The position sets the acceleration.
  • The acceleration changes the velocity.
  • The velocity changes the position.

This uses the staggered method from S09, Eq. 9.16, with a time step h = 0.1 s. In this method, velocities are computed at half-steps:

Step Calculation Result
Half-step velocity v(0.05) = −0.6 + 0.05 × (−2.7) −0.735 m/s
Position x(0.1) = 0.3 + 0.1 × (−0.735) 0.2265 m (exact value: 0.22750)
New acceleration −9 × 0.2265 −2.0385 m/s²
Next half-step velocity v(0.15) = −0.735 − 0.20385 −0.93885 m/s
Position x(0.2) 0.13262 m (exact value: 0.13467)

Accuracy check: I ran the method to t = π/6 s with smaller time steps. Each time the step was halved, the error became about 4 times smaller:

Steps Error at t = π/6 s
5 0.00447 m
10 0.00111 m
20 0.000276 m
40 0.0000688 m

So the steps converge. They give a correct picture without a known formula.

3. Solve exactly and test the solution (M2)

The frequency. Try a cosine, then substitute it into x'' = −9x. That gives ω₀² = k/m = 9, so ω₀ = 3 rad/s. The period is T = 2π/3 ≈ 2.094 s.

The units agree: (N/m)/kg = 1/s². The period does not depend on the amplitude. Four times the mass gives twice the period.

The constants from the starting state. Use x = x₀·cos(3t) + (v₀/ω₀)·sin(3t). With the data:

x(t) = 0.3·cos(3t) − 0.2·sin(3t) (in m)
v(t) = −0.9·sin(3t) − 0.6·cos(3t) (in m/s)

The checks:

Check Result
Equation x'' = −9x exactly, so m·x'' + k·x = 0
Initial values x(0) = 0.3 m ✓. v(0) = −0.6 m/s ✓
Energy E = ½mv² + ½kx² = ½·2·0.36 + ½·18·0.09 = 0.36 + 0.81 = 1.17 J
Energy constant in time dE/dt = v·(m·x'' + k·x) = 0

Compare with the prediction.

  • The amplitude is A = √(0.3² + 0.2²) = 0.3606 m. That is larger than 0.3 m, as predicted.
  • Energy gives the same number: ½kA² = 9 × 0.13 = 1.17 J.
  • The maximum speed is ω₀A = 1.082 m/s, and ½·m·v_max² = 1.17 J. ✓
  • The mass passes x = 0 at t = 0.328 s, moving at its maximum speed.
  • It reaches x = −0.3606 m at t = 0.851 s.

After a quarter cycle (t = π/6 = 0.524 s): x = −0.2 m and v = −0.9 m/s. Energy: 0.81 + 0.36 = 1.17 J ✓. The start values return with their roles swapped:

  • The new position is v₀/ω₀.
  • The new velocity is −ω₀·x₀.

This swap is easiest to see with the circle picture in S21 §21-3. Draw a point that moves around a circle at a constant 3 rad/s. The mass's position is the shadow of that point on one axis. The mass does not move in a circle. The picture only shows the phase. Here the phase angle is atan2(0.2, 0.3) = 0.588 rad (33.7°), so x = 0.3606·cos(3t + 0.588).

Size versus timing. Double both x₀ and v₀ and the amplitude doubles, the energy becomes 4 times larger (4.68 J), and the period stays 2.094 s. The starting data set the size. The ratio k/m sets the timing. A change to the amplitude cannot correct a wrong frequency [S21, §21-2].

4. Core and extensions (M6)

The core of this lesson is sections 1 to 3: the force law → the motion → the checks. This problem has no damping and no drive force, so I did not add them. Those are extensions:

  • Damping is method M4.
  • Drive forces and resonance are method M3.

Tell me if you want either one.

5. Transfer question

Keep x₀ = 0.3 m and v₀ = −0.6 m/s. Change the mass to 8 kg. Before you calculate, predict three things: does the period go up or down? Does the amplitude grow or shrink? Does the energy change?

Answer key
  • ω₀ = 1.5 rad/s and T = 4.189 s. Four times the mass doubles the period.
  • x = 0.3·cos(1.5t) − 0.4·sin(1.5t), so A = 0.5 m. The same speed now carries the mass farther, because the return force works on more mass.
  • E = 1.44 + 0.81 = 2.25 J. The kinetic energy at the start is 4 times larger.
